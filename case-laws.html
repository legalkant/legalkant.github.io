Indian Case Laws

Study important Indian judgments, constitutional decisions and leading legal principles using authoritative sources.

Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala

Constitutional Law · (1973) 4 SCC 225

A landmark constitutional decision associated with the Basic Structure Doctrine and the limits of Parliament's constitutional amending power.

Official Supreme Court Source

Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India

Fundamental Rights · (1978) 1 SCC 248

An important Article 21 decision concerning personal liberty and constitutional protection of individual rights.

Supreme Court Source

Minerva Mills Ltd. v. Union of India

Constitutional Law · (1980) 3 SCC 625

A leading constitutional-amendment case concerning the Basic Structure Doctrine and constitutional limitations.

Supreme Court Source

D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal

Criminal Justice · (1997) 1 SCC 416

Important decision concerning safeguards relating to arrest, detention and protection of individual liberty.

Supreme Court Source

Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan

Constitutional / Labour Law · (1997) 6 SCC 241

Important decision concerning safeguards against sexual harassment at the workplace.

Supreme Court Source

Shreya Singhal v. Union of India

Constitutional / Technology Law · (2015) 5 SCC 1

Important free-speech decision concerning Section 66A of the Information Technology Act, 2000.

Supreme Court Source

Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India

Privacy · (2017) 10 SCC 1

Constitution Bench decision recognising privacy as a constitutionally protected right.

Supreme Court Source

Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India

Constitutional Law · (2018) 10 SCC 1

Constitution Bench decision concerning constitutional rights and Section 377 IPC as applied to consensual same-sex relations between adults.

Supreme Court Source

Joseph Shine v. Union of India

Constitutional / Criminal Law · (2019) 3 SCC 39

Constitution Bench decision concerning Section 497 IPC and constitutional principles relating to adultery.

Supreme Court Source

Case Law Research Note

Always read the actual judgment before relying upon a case in an assignment, legal opinion, pleading or other legal work. A case summary should not be treated as a substitute for the judgment.
