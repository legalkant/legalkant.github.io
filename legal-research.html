Legal Research

Learn how to find legislation, judgments, legal authorities and reliable legal materials for academic and legal research.

What is Legal Research?

Legal research is the systematic process of identifying, locating, reading, analysing and applying legal authorities relevant to a particular legal issue. A proper research process normally begins with identifying the legal question and then examining the applicable legislation, rules, judicial decisions and other reliable authorities.

📜 Statutory Research

Find the legislation applicable to a legal issue and examine the relevant provisions.

  • Identify the relevant Act
  • Check the Act number and year
  • Locate relevant sections
  • Check amendments
  • Read definitions and exceptions

⚖️ Case Law Research

Search judicial decisions to understand how courts have interpreted and applied legal provisions.

  • Search by party name
  • Search by case number
  • Search by judgment date
  • Search by legal issue
  • Read the complete judgment

How to Conduct Legal Research

A practical research workflow for law students.

1

Identify the Legal Problem

Start with the facts and identify the precise legal question that needs to be answered.

2

Find the Applicable Law

Identify the relevant Constitution provision, Act, section, rule, regulation or notification.

3

Check the Current Version

Check whether the legislation has been amended, repealed, substituted or otherwise modified.

4

Find Relevant Judgments

Search judgments dealing with the legal provision, issue or factual situation.

5

Read the Judgment Carefully

Identify the facts, issues, arguments, applicable law, reasoning and final decision.

6

Verify the Authority

Whenever possible, verify the legislation and judgment against an official or authoritative source.

7

Prepare Your Legal Analysis

Connect the applicable law and judicial reasoning with the facts and legal issue under examination.

Legal Research Checklist

Before completing a research assignment or legal note, check the following points.

  • Have I clearly identified the legal issue?
  • Have I identified the relevant legislation?
  • Have I checked the relevant section or provision?
  • Have I checked whether the law has been amended?
  • Have I searched for relevant judgments?
  • Have I read the actual judgment instead of relying only on a summary?
  • Have I verified the case name and citation?
  • Have I used reliable primary sources wherever possible?
  • Have I distinguished the facts of the case from the legal principle decided by the court?
  • Have I properly recorded the sources used in my research?

Primary & Secondary Legal Sources

Primary Sources

Primary legal authorities are the materials that establish or officially record the law.

  • Constitution
  • Acts / Statutes
  • Rules and Regulations
  • Notifications
  • Judgments and Orders

Secondary Sources

Secondary materials help explain, analyse or discuss the law.

  • Textbooks
  • Commentaries
  • Law journals
  • Research papers
  • Legal articles
Research Note: LegalKant is an educational legal-learning platform. Laws, amendments, notifications and judicial decisions can change over time. Always verify important legal information against the latest official or authoritative source before relying upon it for legal advice, litigation, filing or professional work.
