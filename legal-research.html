Legal Research

A practical guide for law students to find legislation, judgments, legal authorities and reliable legal research materials.

What is Legal Research?

Legal research is the systematic process of identifying, locating, reading, analysing and applying legal authorities relevant to a particular legal question. A proper research process generally requires examination of applicable legislation, rules, regulations, judicial decisions and other reliable legal materials.

Areas of Legal Research

Explore different types of research used in legal education and legal practice.

📜 Statutory Research

Research Indian legislation through Government sources and identify the provisions applicable to a legal issue.

  • Search Acts and legislation
  • Search sections and provisions
  • Identify Acts by name, year or number
  • Check amendments and legislative material
  • Research rules and regulations
  • Verify the applicable statutory provision

🔎 Legal Issue Research

Learn how to convert facts into a precise legal question and identify the law and authorities required to answer it.

How to identify a legal issue:

  • Read and identify the material facts
  • Identify the parties and their legal relationship
  • Determine the rights and obligations involved
  • Identify the relevant area of law
  • Frame a specific legal question
  • Find the applicable statutory provision
  • Search relevant judicial decisions
  • Compare the facts with relevant authorities

Example:

Instead of asking: "What is property law?" frame a specific legal issue such as:

"Whether a particular transfer of property is legally valid under the applicable law?"

The researcher can then identify the applicable statute, provisions, rules and judicial decisions.

How to Conduct Legal Research

Follow a structured research process instead of relying on random search results.

1

Identify the Legal Problem

Understand the facts and identify the precise legal question that needs to be answered.

2

Frame the Legal Issue

Convert the facts into a specific legal question capable of being answered through legal authorities.

3

Find the Applicable Law

Identify the Constitution provision, Act, section, rule, regulation or notification relevant to the issue.

4

Check the Current Law

Check whether the relevant legislation has been amended, substituted, repealed or otherwise changed.

5

Search Judicial Decisions

Search for judgments dealing with the relevant statutory provision, constitutional issue or legal question.

6

Read the Judgment

Read the actual judgment and identify the facts, issues, arguments, reasoning and final decision.

7

Verify the Authority

Verify important legislation and judgments through official or authoritative sources before relying on them.

8

Prepare the Legal Analysis

Apply the identified legal principles to the facts and explain the reasoning supported by the authorities.

Primary & Secondary Legal Sources

Understanding the difference between primary and secondary sources is an important part of legal research.

⚖️ Primary Legal Sources

Primary sources are official legal authorities that establish or record the law.

  • Constitution
  • Acts / Statutes
  • Rules and Regulations
  • Notifications
  • Government Orders
  • Judicial Judgments and Orders

📚 Secondary Legal Sources

Secondary sources explain, analyse or discuss primary legal authorities.

  • Textbooks
  • Legal Commentaries
  • Law Journals
  • Research Papers
  • Legal Articles
  • Academic Publications

✅ Legal Research Checklist

Before completing a research assignment, legal note or research paper, check the following.

  • Have I clearly identified the legal issue?
  • Have I identified the applicable legislation?
  • Have I checked the relevant section or provision?
  • Have I checked whether the law has been amended?
  • Have I searched for relevant judicial decisions?
  • Have I read the actual judgment instead of relying only on a summary?
  • Have I verified the case name and citation?
  • Have I used primary sources wherever possible?
  • Have I separated the facts of the case from the legal principle decided by the court?
  • Have I properly recorded all sources used in the research?

💡 Legal Research Tips for Law Students

Start With the Law

First identify the applicable legal provision before depending heavily on summaries or general internet articles.

Read the Original Judgment

A case summary may be useful for orientation, but important research should be verified against the actual judgment.

Verify Citations

Check the case title, court, date, case number and available citation before using a case as legal authority.

Check Amendments

Do not automatically rely on an old version of legislation. Check the latest available official material.

Compare Authorities

Where relevant, examine multiple judicial decisions and distinguish cases based on their facts and legal issues.

Keep Research Notes

Record important sections, cases, quotations, reasoning and source details while conducting research.
